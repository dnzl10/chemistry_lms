Theory & Mathematical Principles

Theoretical Foundations & Mathematical Models

Core physics governing Rutherford scattering, Coulomb electrostatic repulsion, and the discovery of the atomic nucleus.

Rutherford's 1911 Discovery

In 1911, Ernest Rutherford directed a beam of alpha particles (He2+) at a thin sheet of gold foil. Under Thomson's plum pudding model, all particles were expected to shoot straight through with minimal deflection.

Instead, roughly 1 in 8,000 particles bounced straight backward (scattering angle θ > 90°), proving that the positive charge and almost all the mass of the atom are concentrated in an ultra-dense, tiny central core: the nucleus.

Coulomb Repulsion Force

Alpha particles carry a charge of q1 = +2e. The target nucleus carries a positive charge of q2 = +Ze. The repulsive force follows Coulomb's inverse-square law:

F = (1 / 4πε0) • (2Z e2) / r2
  • Z: Atomic number (number of protons)
  • e: Elementary charge (1.602 × 10-19 C)
  • r: Distance between alpha particle and target nucleus

Scattering Angle vs Impact Parameter

The deflection angle θ depends inversely on the impact parameter b (the perpendicular distance of closest approach if undeflected):

cot(θ / 2) = (4πε0 Ek / Z e2) • b
  • As impact parameter b → 0, head-on collision occurs, and θ → 180° (full backscattering).
  • As impact parameter b increases, deflection angle rapidly approaches 0°.

Distance of Closest Approach

For a direct head-on collision (b = 0), all initial kinetic energy Ek converts to electric potential energy at the turning point rmin:

rmin = (1 / 4πε0) • (2Z e2) / Ek

For a 7.7 MeV alpha particle on gold (Z = 79), rmin ≈ 3.0 × 10-14 m. This set the very first physical upper bound on the radius of the atomic nucleus!

Thomson's Plum Pudding Failure

In Thomson's model, positive charge was distributed over a volume of radius R ≈ 10-10 m. The maximum electric field at the surface of this sphere is:

Emax = (1 / 4πε0) • (Z e) / R2

Because R is four orders of magnitude larger than a nucleus, the electric field is 108 times weaker! An alpha particle passing through would experience maximum deflection of less than 0.05°, making backscattering physically impossible under Thomson's model.

Rutherford Differential Cross Section

The number of alpha particles scattered at angle θ per unit solid angle is given by Rutherford's famous formula:

N(θ) ∝ 1 / sin4(θ / 2)

This steep inverse fourth-power relationship explains why almost all particles pass straight through undeflected, while backscattering at large angles is exceedingly rare.
