Theory & Mathematical Principles

Foundational Principles & Thermodynamic Laws

Kinetic molecular theory, microscopic states of matter, and gas laws.

Particle Nature of Matter

All matter is composed of microscopic particles (atoms or molecules) in constant, random motion. Temperature is directly proportional to average translational kinetic energy:

⟨Ek⟩ = (3 / 2) • kB T

Thermal energy directly determines whether attractive forces keep particles locked in place (solid), free to flow (liquid), or completely detached (gas).

The Three Classical States

  • Solid: Fixed shape and volume; particles vibrate around fixed lattice points.
  • Liquid: Fixed volume, conforms to container shape; particles slide and tumble past each other.
  • Gas: Variable shape and volume, fills entire container; particles move rapidly and independently.

Boyle's Law & Pressure

At constant temperature, the pressure of a given mass of gas is inversely proportional to its volume:

P1 V1 = P2 V2

Pushing the piston down halves the container volume, which doubles the rate at which gas molecules collide with the container walls, doubling the measured pressure.

Phase Transitions & Latent Heat

Phase transitions occur at specific temperatures under constant pressure:

  • Melting / Freezing: Solid ⇌ Liquid
  • Vaporization / Condensation: Liquid ⇌ Gas
  • Sublimation / Deposition: Solid ⇌ Gas

During a phase change, heat added supplies latent heat (Q = m • L) to break intermolecular attractions rather than increasing temperature.

Why Ice Floats on Water

Unlike neon or argon, water expands upon freezing:

ρ(ice) ≈ 0.917 g/cm3 < ρ(liquid water) ≈ 1.000 g/cm3

Hydrogen bonding holds water molecules in an open, rigid hexagonal lattice with wide internal cavities, making ice less dense than liquid water.

The Triple Point

The Triple Point is the unique temperature and pressure at which solid, liquid, and gas phases stably coexist in thermodynamic equilibrium.

Water: T = 273.16 K (0.01 °C), P = 0.006 atm (611.65 Pa)

At pressures below the triple point, a substance cannot exist as a liquid at all and sublimes directly between solid and gas.
