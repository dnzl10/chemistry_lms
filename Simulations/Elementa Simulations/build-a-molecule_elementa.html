Theory & Mathematical Principles

Chemical Bonding & Molecular Architecture Theory

Fundamental principles of covalent valence, electronegativity, VSEPR geometries, and Lewis electron sharing.

Covalent Bonding & Octet Rule
Nonmetal atoms share electron pairs to attain stable valence electron configurations resembling noble gases. Hydrogen obeys the duet rule (max 1 bond), Carbon forms 4 bonds (tetravalent), Nitrogen forms 3 bonds (trivalent), Oxygen forms 2 bonds (divalent), and Halogens (F, Cl) form 1 single bond.
Valence Bonds: H=1, C=4, N=3, O=2, F/Cl=1
VSEPR Theory & 3D Shapes
Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion (VSEPR) states that electron pairs around a central atom maximize distance to minimize electrostatic repulsion.

• Linear (180°): CO₂, C₂H₂, BeCl₂
• Bent (~104.5°): H₂O, H₂S, SO₂
• Trigonal Planar (120°): BF₃, CH₂O
• Trigonal Pyramidal (~107°): NH₃, PH₃
• Tetrahedral (109.5°): CH₄, SiH₄, CCl₄
θ(Tetrahedral) = 109.47° | θ(H₂O) = 104.5°
Bond Order & Saturation
Atoms can share 1, 2, or 3 electron pairs:
• Single Bond (σ): Longest, lowest bond energy (e.g., H-H, C-C).
• Double Bond (σ + π): Intermediate length and strength (e.g., O=O, C=C in ethylene).
• Triple Bond (σ + 2π): Shortest, highest bond energy (e.g., N≡N, H-C≡C-H in acetylene).
Bond Energy: N≡N (945 kJ/mol) >> O=O (498 kJ/mol) >> H-H (436 kJ/mol)
Stoichiometry & Conservation of Mass
In chemical reactions, atoms are neither created nor destroyed, only rearranged into new molecular combinations. Balancing stoichiometric equations requires equal counts of each atomic species on reactant and product sides.
2 H₂ + O₂ ➔ 2 H₂O  |  CH₄ + 2 O₂ ➔ CO₂ + 2 H₂O
