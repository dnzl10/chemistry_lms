Cellular Transport Theory & Mathematical Models

1. The Fluid Mosaic Model

The plasma membrane comprises an amphipathic phospholipid bilayer. Polar phosphate head groups face the aqueous extracellular and intracellular fluids, while hydrophobic fatty acyl chains orient inward, forming an impermeable energetic barrier to hydrophilic ions and macromolecules.

2. Fick's First Law of Simple Diffusion

J = -P × (C_inside - C_outside) = -D × (dC / dx)

Where J is net flux, P is membrane permeability, and dC/dx is concentration gradient. Small non-polar molecules like O₂ and CO₂ partition freely into the hydrophobic core.

3. Facilitated Diffusion & Saturation

Channel proteins (leakage, ligand-gated, voltage-gated) provide a water-filled pore with a selectivity filter. Transport rate follows Michaelis-Menten kinetics:

v = (V_max × [S]) / (K_m + [S])

4. Primary vs Secondary Active Transport

Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase (Primary): Couples direct ATP hydrolysis to electrogenic transport (3 Na⁺ out, 2 K⁺ in), creating the negative resting potential and ion gradients.
Na⁺/Glucose SGLT (Secondary): Uses symport coupling to drive glucose against its chemical gradient powered by downhill Na⁺ influx.
