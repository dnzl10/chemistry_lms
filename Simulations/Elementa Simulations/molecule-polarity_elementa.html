Theory & Mathematical Principles

Theory & Formulas

Comprehensive reference on chemical bond polarity, Pauling electronegativities, vector dipole moments, VSEPR molecular geometry, and electrostatic potentials.

Pauling Electronegativity & Bond Character
Electronegativity (χ) quantifies an atom's ability to attract shared valence electrons in a chemical bond. The difference Δχ = |χA − χB| determines bond character:
Δχ < 0.4 (Nonpolar Covalent)  |  0.4 ≤ Δχ < 1.7 (Polar Covalent)  |  Δχ ≥ 1.7 (Ionic)
Dipole Moment (μ) & The Debye Unit
A dipole moment arises when partial electrical charges δ⁺ and δ⁻ are separated by bond distance d. In chemistry, dipole moments are measured in Debyes (1 D = 3.33564 × 10⁻³⁰ C·m):
μ⃗ = q · r⃗ = δ · d   [Debye, D]
Vector Dipole Sum & Molecular Symmetry
A molecule containing polar bonds can be nonpolar overall if its 3D molecular geometry is symmetric, causing individual bond dipole vectors to cancel out:
μ⃗net = ∑ μ⃗i  ⟹  In CO₂ (linear) or CCl₄ (tetrahedral): μ⃗net = 0⃗
Torque & Alignment in External Electric Fields
When placed in an external uniform electric field E⃗, a polar molecule experiences an electrostatic torque τ⃗ that rotates its dipole into alignment with the field lines:
τ⃗ = μ⃗ × E⃗,   U = −μ⃗ · E⃗ = −μ · E · cos θ
Molecular Electrostatic Potential (MEP)
The electrostatic potential surface color-codes the charge distribution across the molecule: Red denotes electron-rich, negatively charged nucleophilic regions; Blue denotes electron-poor, positively charged electrophilic regions; Green denotes electroneutrality.
V(r) = ∑ (ZA / |r − RA|) − ∫ (ρ(r') / |r − r'|) d³r'
Intermolecular Forces & Bulk Physical Properties
Molecular polarity governs macroscopic boiling points, vapor pressures, and solubility. Polar molecules experience permanent dipole-dipole attractions and hydrogen bonding, demanding higher thermal energy to vaporize than nonpolar analogs.
Like Dissolves Like: Polar solvents (water) dissolve polar/ionic solutes.
