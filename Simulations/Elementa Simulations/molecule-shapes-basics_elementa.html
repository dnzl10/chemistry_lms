Theory & Mathematical Principles

Basic VSEPR Theory & Polyhedral Geometries

Reference guide for foundational VSEPR shapes with 0 lone pairs on the central atom.

1. The Five Canonical Shapes

When a central atom has no non-bonding lone pairs, its molecular geometry matches its electron geometry exactly. Electron domains spread symmetrically into five regular geometric polyhedra:

  • 2 Domains (AX₂): Linear (180°)
  • 3 Domains (AX₃): Trigonal Planar (120°)
  • 4 Domains (AX₄): Tetrahedral (109.5°)
  • 5 Domains (AX₅): Trigonal Bipyramidal (90°, 120°, 180°)
  • 6 Domains (AX₆): Octahedral (90°, 180°)

2. Bond Multiplicity Rule

In VSEPR theory, double and triple bonds contain multiple electron pairs, but because they are constrained between the same two atomic nuclei, they act as a single electron domain when determining overall geometry.

For example, Carbon Dioxide ($O=C=O$) has two double bonds ($AX_2$), so it is completely linear (180°), identical in geometry to Beryllium Chloride ($Cl-Be-Cl$).

4. Valence Bond Theory & Orbital Hybridization

Hybrid orbitals form from the linear combination of atomic s, p, and d wavefunctions to point directly toward the bonded ligand atoms:

  • sp: Linear mixing of 1 s and 1 p orbital (180°)
  • sp²: Trigonal mixing of 1 s and 2 p orbitals (120°)
  • sp³: Tetrahedral mixing of 1 s and 3 p orbitals (109.5°)
  • sp³d: Expanded octet involving 1 d orbital (90°/120°)
  • sp³d²: Six octahedral equivalent lobes (90°)

5. Complete Vector Cancellation

Each bond in molecules like $CF_4$ or $SF_6$ is strongly polar due to large differences in electronegativity ($Deltachi > 1.5$). However, because the bonds are symmetrically distributed in 3D space, the vector sum of dipole moments is identically zero:

vec(μ)_net = Σ vec(μ)_i = 0

Thus, symmetric molecules with identical ligand atoms possess no permanent dipole moment.
