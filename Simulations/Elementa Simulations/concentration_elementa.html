Theory & Mathematical Principles

Aqueous Solutions & Concentration Theory

Fundamental physical principles of dissolution, molarity, dilution equations, and dynamic solubility equilibrium.

Molarity (M) Definition
Molarity ($M$), or molar concentration, is defined as the number of moles of dissolved solute per liter of total solution. It is the primary concentration metric used across quantitative chemical stoichiometry.
M = n / V  (mol / L)
The Dilution Equation
When solvent is added to an existing solution, the amount of solute remains strictly constant while total volume increases, causing concentration to decrease proportionally.
M₁ × V₁ = M₂ × V₂
Solubility & Saturation Limit
A solution is saturated when it contains the maximum concentration of dissolved solute in dynamic thermodynamic equilibrium with solid precipitate. Adding further solute causes immediate crystallization at the bottom of the beaker.
[Solute] = S_limit  |  Excess forms solid precipitate
Evaporation Dynamics
Evaporation removes solvent water while leaving solute behind in the beaker. This increases concentration until the saturation limit is reached, after which solid precipitate crystallizes out as volume continues to drop.
V(t) = V₀ − r_evap × t
