Theory & Mathematical Principles

Subatomic Physics & Atomic Theory

Essential laws governing nuclear stability, isotopic composition, Coulomb electrostatic interactions, and quantum energy shells.

Atomic Number (Z) & Identity
The atomic number (Z) equals the number of protons in an atom's nucleus. It uniquely defines the chemical identity of the element. No two elements possess the same number of protons across all 118 recognized elements.
Element Identity = Z = Number of Protons (1 to 118)
Mass Number (A) & Isotopes
Protons and neutrons each have an approximate atomic mass of 1 u. The total mass number (A) is the sum of protons and neutrons. Isotopes are atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons (e.g., ¹H, ²H deuterium, ³H tritium, ²³⁵U vs ²³⁸U).
A = Z + N  |  Neutrons = A − Z
Net Charge & Ionization
Protons carry a +1 fundamental charge, electrons carry −1, and neutrons are electrically neutral. When protons equal electrons, the atom is neutral. If electrons are lost, a positive cation forms; if electrons are gained, a negative anion forms.
Net Charge = Protons − Electrons
Bohr Electron Energy Shells
Electrons occupy discrete quantized energy levels around the nucleus. The maximum capacity for energy level n is given by 2n²:

• Shell 1 (n=1): Max 2 electrons
• Shell 2 (n=2): Max 8 electrons
• Shell 3 (n=3): Holds up to 18 electrons
• Shell 4 (n=4): Holds up to 32 electrons
Max Electrons per Shell = 2n²  (2, 8, 18, 32)
