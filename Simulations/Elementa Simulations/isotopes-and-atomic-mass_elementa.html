Theory & Mathematical Principles

Theory & Formulas

Fundamental physics and chemistry principles governing nuclear structure, isotopes, and atomic mass determination.

Definition of Isotopes
Isotopes are atoms of the same chemical element having the same number of protons (atomic number Z), but differing numbers of neutrons (neutron number N). Consequently, isotopes share identical chemical properties but possess distinct mass numbers (A = Z + N).
AZX   where A = Z + N
Unified Atomic Mass Unit (u or amu)
One unified atomic mass unit is defined as exactly 1/12 of the mass of a single unbound, ground-state Carbon-12 (¹²C) atom.
1 u = 1.660539 × 10⁻²⁷ kg = (1/12) · m(¹²C)
Weighted Average Atomic Mass
The atomic weight listed on the periodic table represents the weighted average mass of all naturally occurring isotopes of that element, weighted according to their fractional natural abundance (fi = % / 100):
m̄ = ∑ (fi · mi) = f₁m₁ + f₂m₂ + ... + fkmk
Mass Spectrometry Principle
A mass spectrometer ionizes atoms and accelerates them through an electric potential into a perpendicular magnetic field B. The Lorentz force bends ions into a circular path of radius r, cleanly separating isotopes by their mass-to-charge ratio m/q:
r = (m · v) / (q · B)  ⟹  m / q = (B² · r²) / (2 · V)
