Theory & Mathematical Principles

Theory & Formulas

Fundamental chemical principles governing molar concentration, solubility product, Beer-Lambert spectrophotometry, and aqueous solution preparation.

Definition of Molarity (M)
Molarity (also termed molar concentration) is defined as the amount of solute in moles divided by the total volume of solution in liters. It expresses how densely concentrated solute molecules or ions are in the solution.
M = nsolute / Vsolution   [mol/L or M]
Saturation & Precipitation Threshold
A solution is saturated when no more solute can dissolve at that temperature. Adding further solute causes solid crystals to precipitate at the bottom of the vessel. The dissolved concentration remains clamped at the saturation limit Csat.
ndissolved = min(n, Csat · V),   nprecipitate = max(0, n − ndissolved)
Dilution Law (Conservation of Moles)
Adding solvent (water) increases the volume without altering the moles of dissolved solute (n₁ = n₂). Consequently, the initial and final states are related by the conservation equation M₁V₁ = M₂V₂.
M₁ · V₁ = M₂ · V₂  ⟹  M₂ = M₁ · (V₁ / V₂)
Beer-Lambert Color Absorbance Law
The optical absorbance A and color intensity of a colored transition metal solution are directly proportional to molar concentration c and light path length b:
A = ε · b · c = −log₁₀(I / I₀)
Electrolytic Conductivity
Solutes that dissociate completely into mobile cations and anions in aqueous solution (strong electrolytes like CuSO₄, CoCl₂, KMnO₄) conduct electricity via ion migration, causing a conductivity probe bulb to glow brightly. Non-electrolytes (like sugar drink mix) do not conduct.
σ = ∑ zi · F · μi · Ci   (Electrical Conductivity)
Mass to Molarity Conversion
In experimental chemistry, solute is measured on an analytical balance in grams. The mass m is converted to moles via molar mass Mmolar, enabling accurate solution preparation:
n = m / Mmolar,   M = m / (Mmolar · V)
