Acid-Base Theory & Mathematical Principles

1. Brønsted-Lowry Acid-Base Definition

An acid is a proton (H⁺) donor, while a base is a proton acceptor. In aqueous solution, transferred protons immediately hydrate to form hydronium ions: H⁺ + H₂O → H₃O⁺.

2. Autoionization of Water & Kw

Kw = [H₃O⁺] × [OH⁻] = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ (at 25°C)
pH + pOH = 14.00

3. Weak Acid Equilibrium & Ka

For a weak acid HA: HA (aq) + H₂O (l) ⇌ H₃O⁺ (aq) + A⁻ (aq)

Ka = ([H₃O⁺][A⁻]) / [HA]
[H₃O⁺] = (-Ka + √(Ka² + 4(Ka·C₀ + Kw))) / 2

4. Electrical Conductivity

Electrolytic conductivity is directly proportional to the total concentration and mobility of dissolved ions: I ∝ ([H₃O⁺] + [OH⁻] + [A⁻] + [M⁺]). Strong acids and bases are strong electrolytes (intense light bulb radiance); weak acids and bases are weak electrolytes (faint glow).
