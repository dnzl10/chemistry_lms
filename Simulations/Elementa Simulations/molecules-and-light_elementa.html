Question 1 of 5
Why does microwave radiation cause water (H₂O) and carbon monoxide (CO) molecules to rotate, but passes straight through nitrogen (N₂) and oxygen (O₂)?
Explanation: Microwave photons match the rotational energy levels of molecules. To absorb microwave radiation, a molecule must possess a permanent dipole moment (μ ≠ 0) so that the photon's electric field can exert a rotational torque. Nonpolar homonuclear diatomics (N2, O2) have zero dipole moment and cannot rotate.
Question 2 of 5
Carbon dioxide (CO₂) is nonpolar in its linear ground state. Why can it still absorb infrared radiation and act as an active greenhouse gas?
Explanation: The quantum selection rule for IR absorption requires that the dipole moment change during the vibration (dμ/dQ ≠ 0). When CO2 bends or stretches asymmetrically, its center of negative charge shifts away from the positive carbon nucleus, creating a transient dipole that readily absorbs IR photons.
Question 3 of 5
What specific process occurs when stratospheric ozone (O₃) absorbs an energetic ultraviolet (UV) photon?
Explanation: Ultraviolet photons carry sufficient quantum energy (E = hν) to exceed the dissociation energy of ozone's weaker resonance bond. Absorption causes photolysis (O3 + hν → O2 + O), safely dissipating the harmful UV radiation as kinetic and thermal energy.
Question 4 of 5
Which gas absorbs visible photons and enters an excited electronic state (exhibiting a visible glow before re-emitting the photon)?
Explanation: Nitrogen Dioxide (NO₂) has an unpaired valence electron (radical) with low-lying electronic energy levels that precisely match the energy of visible blue-violet photons. Absorption of visible light gives NO₂ its characteristic reddish-brown color in photochemical smog.
Question 5 of 5
Which of the following correctly ranks the four photon bands from lowest energy to highest energy, along with their primary molecular transition?
Explanation: Energy is inversely proportional to wavelength (E = hc / λ). Microwaves (cm) have lowest energy, causing molecular rotation; Infrared (μm) excites vibrations; Visible light excites valence electrons; and Ultraviolet (nm) has highest energy, breaking chemical bonds.
Theory & Mathematical Principles

Molecular Spectroscopy & Photochemistry Reference

Comprehensive guide to quantum transitions, selection rules, atmospheric absorption, and greenhouse physics.

1. The Planck-Einstein Relation

Photons carry quantized packets of electromagnetic energy directly proportional to their frequency and inversely proportional to their wavelength:

E = hν = hc / λ
  • h: Planck's constant (6.626 × 10-34 J·s)
  • c: Speed of light (2.998 × 108 m/s)
  • λ: Wavelength in vacuum

2. Quantum Transition Hierarchy

Different regions of the electromagnetic spectrum correspond to quantized transitions between discrete molecular energy states:

  • Microwave (10⁻³ – 10⁻¹ m): Quantized rotational transitions (ΔJ = ±1). Requires permanent dipole.
  • Infrared (10⁻⁶ – 10⁻⁴ m): Quantized vibrational transitions (Δv = ±1). Requires dynamic dipole change.
  • Visible (400 – 750 nm): Outer valence electron excitations between molecular orbitals (HOMO → LUMO).
  • Ultraviolet (10 – 400 nm): High-energy electronic transitions and photodissociation (bond breaking).

4. The Greenhouse Effect & Thermal Scattering

Solar radiation arrives at Earth predominantly in the visible and UV spectrum, to which N2, O2, CO2, and H2O are largely transparent. Earth's surface absorbs this energy and re-emits it as long-wavelength infrared (terrestrial blackbody radiation, peak ≈ 10 μm).

Greenhouse gases (CO2, H2O, CH4) absorb these outgoing IR photons and re-radiate them isotropically in all directions—including back toward the surface—trapping thermal energy in the troposphere.

5. Stratospheric Ozone & The Chapman Cycle

In the stratosphere, ozone acts as a vital protective umbrella by absorbing high-energy UV-B and UV-C radiation that would otherwise damage DNA and organic molecules:

O₃ + hν (UV) → O₂ + O•

The free oxygen radical quickly recombines with another oxygen molecule (O• + O2 + M → O3 + M), reforming ozone and converting damaging photon energy into benign heat.
