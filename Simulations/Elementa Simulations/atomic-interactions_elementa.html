Lennard-Jones Potential & Molecular Theory

1. The Lennard-Jones (12-6) Model

The Lennard-Jones potential mathematically describes the net interaction energy between two non-bonding neutral atoms or spherical molecules:

V(r) = 4ε [ (σ/r)¹² - (σ/r)⁶ ]

Where ε (epsilon) is the depth of the potential energy well (bond strength), and σ (sigma) is the collision diameter where V(σ) = 0.

2. Interatomic Force Vectors

Force is the negative spatial derivative of the potential energy: F(r) = -dV/dr:

F(r) = (24ε / σ) [ 2(σ/r)¹³ - (σ/r)⁷ ]

Repulsive Term (∝ r⁻¹³): Arises from quantum mechanical Pauli exclusion when electron clouds overlap.
Attractive Term (∝ -r⁻⁷): Arises from London dispersion (fluctuating instantaneous dipoles).

3. Equilibrium Condition (r₀)

Setting F(r) = 0 yields the stable equilibrium separation: r₀ = 2^(1/6) σ ≈ 1.122462 σ. At this distance, the net force is zero and potential energy reaches its global minimum V(r₀) = -ε.
