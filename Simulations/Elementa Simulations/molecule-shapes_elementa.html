Question 1 of 5
Ammonia (NH₃) has 3 single bonds and 1 lone pair on the central nitrogen atom. What are its electron geometry and molecular geometry?
Explanation: Nitrogen in NH₃ is surrounded by 4 electron domains (3 bonding pairs + 1 lone pair = steric number 4). This gives a Tetrahedral electron geometry. Because one domain is an invisible lone pair, the atoms form a Trigonal Pyramidal shape.
Question 2 of 5
Why is the measured H–O–H bond angle in water (104.5°) smaller than the ideal tetrahedral angle (109.5°)?
Explanation: Non-bonding electron pairs are held closer to the central oxygen nucleus without a second nucleus to constrain them. Consequently, LP–LP and LP–BP repulsions are significantly stronger than BP–BP repulsions, compressing the bond angle from 109.5° down to 104.5°.
Question 3 of 5
In a molecule with 5 electron domains such as SF₄ (AX₄E), why does the lone pair occupy an equatorial position instead of an axial position?
Explanation: Repulsions at 90° are severely destabilizing. An equatorial lone pair has only two 90° neighbors (the two axial atoms), whereas an axial lone pair would have three 90° neighbors (the three equatorial atoms). Thus, lone pairs always occupy equatorial positions in trigonal bipyramidal systems.
Question 4 of 5
What is the molecular geometry and hybridization of the central xenon atom in Xenon Tetrafluoride (XeF₄)?
Explanation: XeF₄ has 4 bonded fluorine atoms and 2 lone pairs on Xe (steric number = 6, Octahedral electron geometry, sp³d² hybridization). To minimize LP–LP repulsion, the two lone pairs sit 180° trans across from each other, leaving the 4 fluorine atoms in a Square Planar configuration.
Question 5 of 5
Which of the following molecules has a Trigonal Planar electron geometry and a Bent molecular geometry?
Explanation: SO₂ has 3 electron domains around sulfur (1 single/resonance bond, 1 double bond, and 1 lone pair = steric number 3). Its electron geometry is Trigonal Planar, and because one position is a lone pair, its molecular shape is Bent (bond angle ~119°).
Theory & Mathematical Principles

VSEPR Theory & Molecular Geometry Reference

Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion (VSEPR) predicts 3D geometric shapes by minimizing electrostatic repulsions between electron clouds.

1. Core Principles of VSEPR Theory

Valence electrons surrounding a central atom reside in localized charge clouds (bonding pairs or lone pairs). Because like charges repel, these clouds position themselves as far apart as possible in 3D space.

  • Steric Number (SN): The total count of bonded atoms plus non-bonding lone pairs attached to the central atom: SN = B + LP.
  • Multiple bonds (double or triple) count as a single electron domain in determining geometry.

2. Electron Pair Repulsion Hierarchy

Non-bonding lone pairs are attracted to only one atomic nucleus and therefore occupy a larger, more diffuse spatial volume than bonding pairs:

LP – LP > LP – BP > BP – BP

This asymmetric repulsion forces bonded pairs closer together, causing real bond angles to compress relative to ideal geometric predictions (e.g. 104.5° in H₂O vs ideal 109.5°).

4. Valence Bond Theory & Orbital Hybridization

Hybridization describes the mathematical mixing of atomic orbitals (s, p, d) to form new degenerate hybrid orbitals oriented toward the electron domains:

  • sp: 1 s + 1 p → 2 linear orbitals (180°)
  • sp²: 1 s + 2 p → 3 trigonal planar orbitals (120°)
  • sp³: 1 s + 3 p → 4 tetrahedral orbitals (109.5°)
  • sp³d: 1 s + 3 p + 1 d → 5 trigonal bipyramidal orbitals
  • sp³d²: 1 s + 3 p + 2 d → 6 octahedral orbitals (90°)

5. Symmetry & Net Molecular Dipole Moment

A molecule can contain highly polar bonds yet have zero net dipole moment if its 3D geometry is highly symmetric, causing individual bond dipole vectors to sum to zero ($sum ec{mu} = 0$):

  • Nonpolar Symmetric: Linear ($CO_2$), Trigonal Planar ($BF_3$), Tetrahedral ($CH_4$), Trigonal Bipyramidal ($PCl_5$), Square Planar ($XeF_4$), Octahedral ($SF_6$).
  • Polar Asymmetric: Bent ($H_2O, SO_2$), Trigonal Pyramidal ($NH_3$), Seesaw ($SF_4$), T-shaped ($ClF_3$), Square Pyramidal ($BrF_5$).
